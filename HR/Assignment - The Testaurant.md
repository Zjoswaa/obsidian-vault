###### Finding
The finding in this text is the observation that the taste of egg is not noticeable in the Caesar salad, this is a difference between the expectation and the actual outcome.
###### Bug:
The bug is the delay in serving salads to the big group of 15 guests due to the cook not having taken into account so many orders at once. This causes the kitchen to not perform entirely to the specifications.
###### User acceptance test:
The user acceptance test is the restaurant serving staff tasting the Caesar salad to get an idea of what customers will think of it. Particularly whether it tastes good, is easy to eat and looks appetizing.
###### Environment-related finding:
The environment-related finding is the difficulty faced by staff in serving a large number of salads because of inadequate preparation for such situations.
###### Security test:
The security test is the inspector from the Food Inspection Department coming to inspect the kitchen ensuring that the kitchen is hygienic and the ingredients are fresh and are kept hygienically.
###### Stakeholders
In this text the stakeholders would be the restaurant owner. There are probably also other stakeholders but they are not mentioned in this text.
###### Stress / Load testing:
Stress / Load testing in this case would be simulating a high amount of orders to evaluate how well the kitchen can handle high demand without sacrificing quality or efficiency.
###### System test:
The system test would be evaluating the whole process of preparing and serving the salad, this not only includes the preparation and serving, but also ingredient sourcing, kitchen maintenance, guest satisfaction, etc.
###### Test basis:
The test basis is the recipe and ingredients list that the the restaurant owner gave the cook. This forms the basis for testing the Caesar salad.
###### Test object:
The test object is the Caesar salad itself, as well as the restaurant's capability of its preparation and serving.
###### Test environment:
The test environment is the restaurant kitchen, dining area, and any other areas where the Caesar salad *exists*.
###### Unit test:
The unit test is checking the freshness and quality of individual ingredients, for example how green and crunchy the lettuce is, whether or not the eggs are expired, and if the tomatoes smell good and are juicy.
###### Unit integration test:
The unit integration test is ensuring that all components of the Caesar salad taste good together and form a good meal together. During this test it was noted that the taste of egg was not noticeable enough.
###### Usability test:
Usability testing is evaluating how easy it is to eat the Caesar salad, as well as how the salad looks on the plate. This could include feedback from kitchen staff, serving staff, and the guests who eat the salad.
